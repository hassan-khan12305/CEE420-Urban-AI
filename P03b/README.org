On the field day the photographs went into the software and came out as an orthomosaic, a surface model and a point cloud. Precept 03b opens the machine between the two. The seven notebooks follow the seven boxes of the lecture's pipeline, photographs, tie points, sparse cloud, dense cloud, mesh, surface model, orthomosaic, and each one reads what the software left behind at that stage, cut to one window of the field and one neighbourhood of photographs, and does the stage's central computation by hand in a few lines beside it. Three of them we run together on Wednesday 30 September, 03b_02, 03b_04 and 03b_07; the other four are shown from the instructor's copies and are self-paced, finished, and shipped with their outputs, so you can read them before you run them. None of it is assessed.

Start with the first cells of =03b_01=. They print which flight the folder holds and what the software read from its first photograph, and if that works your environment is ready for the rest. Then read in order. Every notebook prints the numbers it computes beside the numbers the pipeline computed from the same files, which travel in =data/ground_truth.json=, so nothing is typed twice. The kit was written on the pre-flight of 17 September and is re-run unchanged on the class flight; =data/flight.json= names the flight a notebook was last run on, and where a number belongs to one particular flight the prose says so by name.

| Notebook | What it covers |
|----------+----------------|
| =03b_01_what_the_software_read= | The software's own reading of the photographs, the camera it assumed and the two priors that disagree, the receiver's positions and the accuracy it was told to trust, the orientation it never used, the footprints and the pairs they predict against the pairs the software matched, its own count of photographs per ground cell, the rolling shutter in pixels, and the unknowns of the block before a single point |
| =03b_02_what_a_feature_is= | A corner found by hand with the Harris response and a blob found at its scale with a difference of Gaussians, the software's features of the same photograph with their scale and direction, where they fall against the field, and the 128 numbers of a descriptor that let the same ground point be found from another position |
| =03b_03_from_matches_to_tracks= | The pair matched by descriptor with the ratio test, the candidates that agree on one camera motion by the fundamental matrix and RANSAC, three epipolar lines, every pair of the flight against the footprints' prediction, and pairwise matches joined into tracks by union find, with the track lengths beside the software's |
| =03b_04_the_block_and_its_adjustment= | The reconstruction: cameras and points in the adjustment's own frame, where the software put each camera against the receiver, how it turned each one against the file's yaw, the collinearity equations as ten lines of code and the residuals they leave, the two frames of one block, the camera the flight calibrated and what the prior would have cost, the block counted in unknowns and equations, and a bundle adjustment of eight photographs in scipy that brings perturbed cameras back |
| =03b_05_a_height_for_every_pixel= | Dense matching on one strip of pixels, the correlation search along an epipolar line for a roof corner and a lawn pixel, the software's depth map of one photograph with its confidence, the depth map unprojected into a cloud, and the fused cloud in the window with its density and its views per point |
| =03b_06_mesh_and_surface_model= | A 2.5D mesh from the window's points by Delaunay beside the software's, the surface model by gridding the highest point per cell beside the software's, the ground under it by a progressive morphological filter beside the software's terrain, and hillshades of both |
| =03b_07_the_orthomosaic_by_hand= | One photograph rectified onto the surface model with the collinearity equations run backwards, the roof standing over its footprint, a second photograph on the same grid and the seam between them, the software's mosaic beside the by-hand product, and the same photograph rectified onto the terrain model so that the roof leans again |

The data in =data/= is what the software left behind on this flight, cut to the pinned 50 by 35 m window that P03 uses for its full resolution crop and to a neighbourhood of eight photographs around the one that sees the window best: three photographs and two undistorted copies at 2000 pixels, the features and descriptors of one pair, the robust matches within the neighbourhood, every observation of every reconstructed point, both reconstructions of the block with the calibrated camera and the prior, one depth map, the filtered dense cloud, the mesh, the surface and terrain models and the mosaic over the window, the software's report and its EXIF table, and the outline of the field from OpenStreetMap. All of it is the course's own work under CC BY 4.0, except the outline, which is ODbL 1.0, copyright OpenStreetMap contributors. Every raster carries its source, its licence and its retrieval date in its tags, every array file the same in its own arrays, and sources and licences are listed in =data/README.org=. Nothing that identifies a person is in any file.
